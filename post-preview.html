building teachaid central

2024-09-20

every education platform i have ever used has had a discoverability problem. not in the sense that students cannot find it, but in the sense that students cannot find the right help at the right time. a student who is stuck on quadratic equations at 11pm does not need a directory of tutors with bios and hourly rates; they need a session, now, with someone qualified.

teachaid central was built around that specific moment. it is a coordination layer that skips the browse-search-pick-pay loop and lands the student in front of a verified tutor for the subject they need, on the timeline they need it.

the problem we started with

the initial premise was simpler: match students to tutors. but after talking to a handful of students in accra, a few patterns emerged. first, the search itself was the friction — not the price, not the availability. second, sessions that went well rarely ended; students wanted to keep working with the same tutor for the same subject over a term. third, the "verified" part was doing a lot of silent work: parents cared about it more than students did, and it was the deciding factor when it was present.

so the platform grew three core pieces:

  • a fast matchmaking path for one-off sessions
  • a persistent relationship layer for term-long tutoring
  • a verification badge that parents can trust at a glance

the stack

we built on node.js for the backend, supabase for auth and the data layer, and tailwind for the interface. nothing exotic. the interesting constraints came from the offline-first reality of ghanaian student life: intermittent connectivity, phones with limited storage, and a strong preference for whatsapp as the primary communication channel.

that last one shaped a lot of the design. we ended up treating whatsapp as a first-class notification channel rather than trying to push students into the app.

designing for the tools people already use is often more effective than designing the tool you wish they would use.

what i learned

building teachaid central as a solo engineer taught me two things i would not have learned in a larger team. first, that a clean data model is worth more than any single feature — the whole platform rests on a sessions table that we got right in the first two weeks and have barely touched since. second, that verification is a product, not a checkbox. the reason parents trust the badge is that we made it expensive to earn, and the expense is the point.

the project is still running at teachaid-central-nine.vercel.app and the source is on github.
